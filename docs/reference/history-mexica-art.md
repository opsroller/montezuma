# History: Mexica art, 1502 to 1520

*Reference. A sourced reference set for the art director, technical
artists (3D board, pieces, materials, textures) and UI and graphic
designers: what Mexica art in the reign of Moctezuma II Xocoyotzin (1502
to 1520) looked like, what each piece of evidence shows and does not
show, and who may reuse it. Why the board looks as it does today:
[Art direction](../explanation/art-direction.md). The look each tier must
reach: [Visual target](visual-target.md) and
[About the visual target](../explanation/about-the-visual-target.md).
Where a theme plugs in: [Board themes](board-themes.md) and
[Make a board theme](../how-to/make-a-board-theme.md). Owner direction
that sourced history leads the look:
[Project profile](project-profile.md#product).*

**Reference informs original work. Never trace, copy, photobash or
texture from any image linked here into shipped art.** Only images whose
holder marks them public domain, CC0 or CC BY may be placed in the
project, and each one is logged in
[`game/assets/PROVENANCE.md`](../../game/assets/PROVENANCE.md) first.
This page places no image files.

## Scope

| | |
|---|---|
| People | The Mexica (Tenochca) of Tenochtitlan and their twin city Tlatelolco (under Tenochca rule from 1473); the Nahua altepetl working for or with them: Texcoco and Tlacopan (the Triple Alliance), Azcapotzalco (metalwork), Amantla (featherwork) |
| Place | The Basin of Mexico: a highland lake basin at about 2,240 m with volcanoes, reed marsh, chinampa fields, causeways and dry pine and oak slopes. Not jungle |
| Period | Late Postclassic, reign of Moctezuma II, 1502 to 1520. Earlier work of the same city (Templo Mayor stages about 1440 to 1502) is context and is dated and labelled |
| Social range | Elite (court, nobles, priests), ritual (sacred precinct, offerings, calendar) and everyday (macehualtin, markets, chinampas, ceramics, obsidian, cloth). Women, artisans and merchants included |
| Later witnesses | Colonial painted books (Codex Mendoza about 1541, Florentine Codex 1575 to 1577): form and colour conventions only, always with their date |
| Comparanda | Mixtec gold and the Borgia Group codices, for technique only and labelled as another people's work |
| Out of scope for the look | The Maya and lowland jungle, Teotihuacan and Toltec as settings, colonial life after 1521, the conquest war, neo-Aztec and nationalist imagery, Hollywood jungle temples, and the original game's 1935 Cancún museum framing (a reference for rules and mood only) |

Item ids (AR, OB, DR, MA, CO, IC for the set; NU for material not used)
are used in the brief below. Every item lists its holder's own record.

## Artist brief

### Essentials

1. **Setting.** A bright, dry highland island city on a lake, quartered
   by water and joined to shore by causeways (AR-11, AR-12). Outside the
   sacred precinct the city is low adobe-on-masonry houses round small
   patios with stucco floors (AR-13), and chinampa fields (AR-14,
   modern model). Not rainforest.
2. **Twin temple.** One stepped platform with two parallel west-facing
   stairs to two shrines: Huitzilopochtli on the south, Tlaloc on the
   north. Attested at Tenochtitlan (AR-01) and independently at
   Tlatelolco (AR-07), and drawn in Florentine Codex XI f. 240v (AR-10).
   The 1502 to 1520 building is Stage VII; almost nothing of it stands,
   so its look is carried forward from Stages II to VI (AR-02) at about
   80 m a side and about 45 m high.
3. **Building materials.** Tezontle (red, violet or black porous scoria)
   walls set in mud, basalt slab floors and stairs, lime stucco over
   floors, walls and stairs (AR-04, AR-06). Palace and plaza courts are
   paved with basalt slabs (AR-04, AR-06).
4. **Painted, not grey.** Living buildings and sculpture were stuccoed
   and painted (AR-02, AR-03, CO-01, CO-09). Bare grey stone is how the
   ruins look now. Weathered stone can keep paint in carved recesses
   (inferred).
5. **The stone palette has five colour families only:** hematite red
   (and a darker wine red), goethite ochre (yellow to orange, often
   dominant), Maya-blue turquoise-blue, lime or calcite white, and soot or
   charcoal black (CO-01, CO-02, CO-03, CO-06). Paint is flat, opaque,
   saturated, hard-edged, with no shading or gradient (CO-02, CO-06), so
   painted albedo stays flat and the 3D light does the shading.
6. **Colour carries direction.** North and Tlaloc are blue (cold, rain);
   south and Huitzilopochtli are ochre (hot, sun) (CO-01 p. 103, CO-09).
   The board frame or chamber can use this blue/ochre duality.
7. **Friezes run in repeated horizontal bands** of ochre, red, blue and
   black (CO-03 p. 32, Casa de las Águilas), a sourced basis for frame
   and border ornament. The step-fret (xicalcoliuhqui) is attested on
   feather regalia (DR-10) and is right as a band or frame motif, not as
   structure. Roof edges carry almenas such as the cut-shell form (AR-04).
8. **The board chamber** may be invented, resting on: a bench along the
   wall with relief on its face (AR-05), pilaster or column bases carrying
   a flat roof (AR-04, AR-05), a stucco or basalt-slab floor (AR-04),
   painted stucco walls with polychrome bands, braziers like OB-05 at the
   corners, and daylight from a doorway or patio. Label it invention in
   the art direction.
9. **Precious materials are small and personal.** Gold, greenstone,
   turquoise, obsidian, shell and feathers belong to ornaments, insignia
   and offerings, never to wall cladding (DR-01, DR-02, OB-06 to OB-10,
   DR-05). Gold and feathers mark rulers and nobles (DR-01, DR-02, DR-10).
10. **Stones are ground and polished, never faceted** (MA-05, MA-07).
    Greenstone (chalchihuitl) is mostly serpentine and veined marble:
    opaque to slightly translucent, mottled green to grey-green, with a
    lustrous burnished polish; deep translucent vitreous jadeite is rare
    and often an heirloom (MA-05, MA-06). Shapes are round beads, plaques
    and small figures (MA-04).
11. **Turquoise** is small, irregular, matte to waxy tesserae with visible
    dark resin joints on a carved wood core, paired with red shell, white
    conch and black stone (NU-13 facts). Most of it came from Mesoamerican
    sources, not the US Southwest (MA-01).
12. **Gold** at Tenochtitlan was mostly hammered, cut and embossed sheet
    (OB-08), with cast bells and fasteners (OB-06). Alloys were often
    silver-rich and low in copper, so many pieces read pale yellow, not
    reddish (NU-10 facts); cast work has false filigree modelled in wax
    and an enriched high-karat skin (DR-01, MA-02). PBR: metallic 1, low
    roughness on burnished faces, base colour from pale gold to rich
    yellow.
13. **Obsidian** is near-black glass, mirror-polished when ground
    (OB-14, DR-05) and razor-edged and conchoidal when knapped (OB-18);
    keep a grey-green translucent edge variant (OB-15, inferred).
14. **Feathers** are iridescent quetzal green with turquoise cotinga,
    pink spoonbill and rufous squirrel-cuckoo accents, laid in overlapping
    scales and outlined in gold (DR-10, DR-11, NU-13 facts). Render an
    anisotropic sheen, brighter than the faded survivors.
15. **Everyday life is in the picture.** Black-on-Orange tripod ware with
    thin black linework (OB-19 to OB-23), spindle whorls (OB-24), reed
    mats drawn yellow with a diagonal weave (DR-12), lidded baskets and
    women selling in the market (DR-15), obsidian blades (OB-18), flower
    and animal stamps (IC-02). Market or household props at the board edge
    bring in commoners and women.
16. **Dress follows rank.** Men wear the maxtlatl and a tilmatli tied at
    one shoulder; women the cueitl skirt with a belt and the huipilli
    blouse (DR-12, DR-14, DR-08). Commoners wear plain maguey or cotton
    and wood or bone ornaments (DR-07); nobles add patterned cotton, gold,
    rock crystal or obsidian labrets and ear spools (DR-01 to DR-06).
    Ornaments are small against the face: labrets about 4 to 7 cm, spools
    about 2.5 to 4 cm.
17. **Writing labels things.** A place or name glyph sits beside what it
    names (IC-01, IC-03, DR-12). Never invent glyphs or make ornament look
    like writing.
18. **Label every pre-1502 element** with its stage and ruler (AR-01
    Stage IVb, Axayacatl 1469 to 1481; AR-06 Stage VI, Ahuitzotl 1486 to
    1502; AR-05 about 1430 to 1500). Only the Calmécac Stage VII floor
    (AR-04) falls inside Moctezuma II's reign among the buildings.

### Piece materials: options for the seven kinds

The current set (ruby, carnelian, gold, jade, lapis, amethyst, moonstone,
in [Art direction](../explanation/art-direction.md#pieces-read-by-shape-first))
is mostly not Mexica: faceting is not attested, lapis lazuli and moonstone
were not found in any Mexica record, and carnelian and amethyst were not
verified. These are options for the technical artist, who decides; each
keeps the rule that the seven kinds read apart by silhouette in greyscale.

| Kind | Current | Mexica-attested option | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 red | Ruby, faceted | Red thorny-oyster (Spondylus) shell, polished | NU-13 facts (BM mosaic materials) | attested |
| 1 orange | Carnelian | No Mexica orange stone found. Options: ochre-painted stone, or burnished red-brown clay | OB-05, CO-06 | inferred |
| 2 yellow | Gold sun disc | Cast gold bell or embossed sheet-gold plaque; a large gold disc is not attested | OB-06, OB-08 | attested (disc: unknown) |
| 3 green | Jade drop | Greenstone (serpentine) bead or pendant, burnished | MA-05, OB-13 | attested |
| 4 blue | Lapis, faceted | Turquoise mosaic plaque, matte tesserae with resin joints | MA-01, MA-04, NU-13 facts | attested |
| 5 purple | Amethyst cluster | No purple material found. Options: a recorded departure, or obsidian (black, which would join the dark-value trio) | OB-14, DR-05 | unknown |
| 6 white | Moonstone crescent | Mother-of-pearl teardrop, cut conch shell, or rock crystal | OB-10, OB-09, DR-04 | attested |

### Do

- Build stone as tezontle, basalt and andesite under lime stucco, and
  paint it in the five families (AR-02, AR-04, CO-01).
- Give the twin temple two stairs, a blue north and an ochre south
  (AR-01, AR-07, CO-01).
- Use the Met temple models for a household-shrine silhouette (AR-08).
- Use flat colour inside a black outline for UI and graphics, with the
  richer codex palette (cochineal crimson and pink, turquoise, greens,
  ochre, orange, browns) kept to paper, cloth and UI (CO-07, DR-12).
- Keep the red of stone (iron hematite) distinct from the crimson of
  dyed cloth and UI (cochineal) (CO-06, CO-07, CO-08).
- Scale props by the records: ritual vessels and braziers about 35 cm
  (OB-05), jewels 2 to 9 cm (DR-01, OB-10), stone boxes about 60 to 75 cm
  (OB-03).
- Show wear honestly: obsidian chips at edges, greenstone goes matte where
  handled, mosaic loses tesserae and shows resin, gold wears softly and
  does not rust (OB-14, MA-05, NU-13 facts).

### Avoid

- Jungle: moss, vines, roots, rainforest mist, limestone-white ruins. The
  Basin is a dry highland lake basin (scope; AR-01 to AR-14).
- Maya forms: corbel vaults, roof combs, a single tall stair to one
  shrine, Puuc mosaic facades, Maya glyph blocks (scope).
- Bare grey stone presented as the working temple (AR-03, CO-01).
- Gold-plated or gem-set architecture: no evidence (essential 9).
- Faceted, brilliant-cut, glassy gems; glassy turquoise; translucent
  "jade" everywhere (MA-05, MA-06, MA-07).
- Reddish tumbaga as the default Mexica gold, and Mixtec filigree
  pendants labelled Aztec (DR-03, MA-08, NU-10 facts).
- The US Southwest turquoise trade told as fact (MA-01).
- Codex colours painted 1:1 onto stone, and painted green or purple on
  monuments (CO-01 p. 95, CO-03 p. 17).
- Teotihuacan (Red Temples) or Toltec revival (AR-05) as the normal
  Mexica style (AR-02, AR-05).
- Building forms from the Nuremberg woodcut (AR-11).
- Feather headdresses on everyone, Plains-style war bonnets, gold armour
  (DR-02, DR-10).
- Sexualised costume: ordinary women wore the huipilli; the bare torso
  of goddess sculpture is a sacred convention (DR-08).
- Colonial conventions taken as pre-conquest: Florentine shading and
  landscape, Spanish glosses (AR-10, DR-15).
- Mixing Tlatelolco with Tenochtitlan, or earlier stages with Stage VII,
  without a label (AR-07, essential 18).
- Skulls, blood, sacrificial knives, dismemberment and deity faces as
  decoration, pieces or totems (see Not used).
- Neo-Aztec, Aztec-revival, nationalist and Hollywood imagery; the 1935
  Cancún museum framing (scope).

## Architecture and space

The 1502 to 1520 temple (Stage VII) is almost entirely gone above its platform, so most building evidence is from earlier stages of the same city. Each item is labelled with its stage and ruler. Sites whose funerary deposits, sacrifice reliefs or memorial status are flagged keep only their building fabric here; the flagged parts are under [Not used, and why](#not-used-and-why).

### Consultation and decisions for the owner

Nothing above is approved by any community; this page claims no approval.
If the answer to any item is no, the product leaves that material out and
uses non-figural precious materials and plain ornament.

1. **Human remains and funerary material (NU-01, NU-02, NU-07).**
   Recommended: leave out entirely, which needs no consultation. If a
   distant precinct with a tzompantli is ever wanted, ask INAH's
   Programa de Arqueología Urbana and Nahua cultural institutions first.
2. **Deity likeness on pieces, totems or effects (NU-05, NU-08, DR-08,
   DR-09, OB-12).** Before any deity face or figure enters gameplay, ask
   Nahua scholars and institutions, for example IDIEZ (Instituto de
   Docencia e Investigación Etnológica de Zacatecas) and the Seminario de
   Cultura Náhuatl at UNAM's Instituto de Investigaciones Históricas,
   whether and how; Tlaloc imagery especially, as rain petitions continue.
3. **The tonalpohualli day signs and the Codex Borbonicus (NU-11).** Ask
   the same, and calendar-keeping practitioners, before day signs appear in
   UI or mechanics. Never as fortune-telling.
4. **The national emblem (NU-12).** Get legal advice on Mexico's Ley sobre
   el Escudo, la Bandera y el Himno Nacionales before using the eagle on a
   nopal in any form.
5. **Commercial reproduction of archaeological monuments.** Get legal
   advice on whether Mexico's Ley Federal sobre Monumentos y Zonas
   Arqueológicos, Artísticos e Históricos and INAH authorisation apply to
   new art closely based on a specific monument (not verified here).
6. **Featherwork and mosaics under return claims (NU-13).** Decide
   whether a close likeness of the Penacho or the shields is ever used;
   recommended: materials and technique only. If wanted, consider asking
   Mexico's cultural authorities (INAH).
7. **The teponaztli (NU-09).** It is in the sound palette; decide whether
   it is shown, and if so as a respected instrument, never broken.
8. **Tlatelolco in marketing (NU-04).** Keep game marketing away from the
   1968 memorial.
9. **The site split.** Confirm that building fabric of sites with funerary
   or sacrifice contexts may be used once those contexts are left out.
10. **Credit.** Decide how the scholars this set depends on (the Templo
    Mayor Project team above all) are credited in `CREDITS.md`.
